**The ontology is the software.** On ObjectOS, *your ontology* is the
definition of your business that the runtime executes: one typed,
versioned body of ObjectStack metadata. The runtime derives the database
schema, the REST API, the Console and the MCP tools from it, and enforces
it on every call. This page maps the word onto this site; the full account
is [Business Ontology](https://objectstack.ai/docs/concepts/ontology) in the
ObjectStack docs.

## The core

Six kinds of thing make up the ontology itself:

| Core | What it declares | On this site |
|---|---|---|
| **Objects and fields** | The entities of the business and their typed attributes | [Data Model](/docs/build/data), [Field Types](/docs/reference/field-types) |
| **Relations** | Lookups and master-detail links between objects | [Relationships](/docs/build/data/relationships) |
| **Actions** | The operations a user — or, once its author exposes it, an agent — may invoke on a record | [Actions](/docs/build/interface/actions) |
| **Permissions** | Who may read, create, edit and delete what: permission sets, record access, field-level security | [Permissions](/docs/configure/permissions) |
| **Flows** | The business processes: flows, record triggers, schedules, approvals, workflows | [Automation](/docs/build/automation) |
| **Agent and tool definitions** | The AI agents the app ships with, their skills, and the tools they may call | [Agents](/docs/build/agents) |

## The projections

Views, dashboards, apps and translations are authored as metadata too, and
they pass the same validation gate, but they are **projections** of the
ontology, not part of it. Change the core and they follow; remove one and
the business it showed is unchanged.

| Projection | What it projects | On this site |
|---|---|---|
| **Views** | An object's fields and permissions onto a list, form, kanban or calendar | [Views](/docs/build/interface/views), [Forms](/docs/build/interface/forms) |
| **Dashboards** | Queries over objects onto charts | [Dashboards](/docs/build/interface/dashboards) |
| **Apps** | Objects, views and actions, grouped into a navigable product | [Apps](/docs/build/interface/apps) |
| **Translations** | Every label, into a locale | [Localization](/docs/configure/localization) |

## Where it lives on ObjectOS

One definition, in three forms:

| Form | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Tenant metadata** | What the [AI Builder](/docs/build/ai-builder) and Studio write: live metadata in your deployment. An AI Builder change queues for a person's approval before it applies |
| **Repository source** | What your developers and their coding agent write, with the [AI skills](/docs/build/ai-skills): `objectstack.config.ts` and its metadata files, reviewed as pull requests |
| **Compiled artifact** | What `os compile` produces: `objectstack.json`, which a deployment boots — see [Boot modes](/docs/architecture#boot-modes). Keep every published one ([Backup](/docs/operate/backup)) |

Whichever form you author in, the metadata passes the same
[validation gate](/docs/resources/glossary#validation-gate) before it runs.
On either edition the ontology is yours: it is ordinary ObjectStack
metadata, and you can export it and run it on the open-source ObjectStack
runtime.

## What it is not

- **Not a knowledge ontology.** There is no class inheritance, no axioms
  and no reasoner. The definition is validated, then run.
- **Not a semantic layer over your existing systems.** A semantic layer
  describes a business it does not run. Your ontology is the system: the
  database, API, UI and agent tools are derived from it. Connecting a
  database you already run is possible, read-only by default and early —
  see [Extend Existing Systems](/docs/build/extend-existing-systems).
