On ObjectOS Cloud everything you own belongs to an **organization**: its
environments, its apps and packages, its plan and its members. You create it
when you sign up ([Start on ObjectOS Cloud](/docs/start/cloud)), and you manage
it in the **cloud console** at [cloud.objectos.ai](https://cloud.objectos.ai).

## The cloud console

The console's sidebar is the map of this section:

| Sidebar item | What it holds | Page |
|---|---|---|
| **Home** | The organization at a glance: production environment, apps, members, this week's AI allowance | — |
| **Environments** | Every environment, with its settings and lifecycle | [Environments](/docs/cloud/environments) |
| **Members** | The people in the organization and their pending invitations | this page |
| **Billing** | The plans compared, and the upgrade | [Plans and AI Seats](/docs/cloud/plans-and-seats) |
| **Advanced → Connected Runtimes** | Self-hosted servers linked to this organization | [Cloud Connection](/docs/cloud/cloud-connection) |
| **Advanced → Packages** | The packages your organization has published | [Apps](/docs/cloud/apps) |

Each environment also has a console of its own, at the environment's own
address. That is where your team uses the apps; the cloud console is where you
manage the organization around them.

## The organization slug

The **slug** is the organization's short identifier, chosen at sign-up. It is
also the address of your production environment: the slug, followed by the
platform's domain.

- 4 to 30 characters: lowercase letters, digits and dashes.
- No dash at the start or end, and no two dashes in a row.
- A short list of reserved words is refused (`admin`, `api`, `www`, `docs`,
  `default` and others the platform uses itself).

An owner or admin can change the slug from the organization's record with
**Change Slug**, at most once every 90 days. Every platform subdomain of the
organization is rewritten to the new slug, and the old slug is held for 90
days so nobody else can take it in the meantime. Your own domains
([Domains](/docs/cloud/domains)) are not affected by a slug change.

## Members and invitations

Open **Members** in the sidebar. The page has two tabs: the members, with
their roles, and the invitations still pending.

To add someone, choose **Invite members**, enter their email address and pick
a role. They receive an invitation, and once they accept it they are a member
of the organization.

**The organization's invitation is the only way into its environments.** An
environment has no invitation and no self sign-up of its own: everyone you
invite here can get into the organization's environments, and what they can
do inside one is decided by that environment's
[permissions](/docs/configure/permissions).

From the lists, whoever manages the organization can **change a member's
role**, **remove a member**, and **resend** or **revoke** a pending invitation.

## Roles

A membership role decides what a person can reach in the organization. It is
not a permission set: what someone may do with records inside an environment
comes from that environment's [permission sets and positions](/docs/configure/permissions).

| Role | What it is for | Who can invite to it |
|---|---|---|
| **Owner** | Full control of the organization, its plan and its environments | An owner |
| **Admin** | Manages the organization: members, environments, billing | An owner or admin |
| **Delegated Admin** | Can reach the invitation endpoint without being an admin; where they may place people comes from an admin scope granted separately | An owner or admin |
| **Member** | Uses the organization's environments and apps | Anyone who can invite |
| **External** | A portal user — a supplier's or a customer's — who sees only their own rows and what is explicitly shared with them; never part of the business-unit tree | Anyone who can invite |

Owners and admins are the people this section means by "whoever manages the
organization": they see the plan, the upgrade and the environment actions that
change something. A plain member sees the environments and opens them, but is
not offered actions they could not complete.

## Where sign-in lives

On ObjectOS Cloud every account is a cloud account. ObjectOS Cloud signs a
person in, and the environment receives them: what an environment holds for
each person is **authorization only** — their positions, permission sets and
sharing. Every hosted environment uses this single sign-in by default, on every
plan, so people sign in once with their ObjectOS Cloud account and move between
environments.

**Taking an environment to your own infrastructure.** Because the accounts
live in ObjectOS Cloud and not in the environment, they do not travel with it.
On a self-managed deployment you either issue people new credentials there, or
bind it to your own identity provider so they sign in through it — see
[Authentication](/docs/configure/authentication).
