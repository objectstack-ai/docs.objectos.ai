An **environment** is one running copy of ObjectOS with its own database,
files, apps and address. Your organization's apps live in it, and your team
signs in to it to use them.

**Every organization has exactly one environment, on every plan.** It is the
production environment: the first one you create when you set up the
organization ([Start on ObjectOS Cloud](/docs/start/cloud)), and the only one.
One organization, one environment, many apps: you install and build as many
apps as you need inside it.

| | **The environment** |
|---|---|
| How many | Exactly one per organization, on every plan |
| Address | Your organization's slug on the platform domain, or any other [platform subdomain](/docs/cloud/domains) you choose, free on every plan |
| Idle | Never archived for inactivity |
| What it is for | The apps your team uses |

A second environment cannot be created. Plans differ in what the environment
holds and allows (its [storage](/docs/cloud/storage-and-limits), its
[AI allowance](/docs/cloud/ai-credits-and-limits)), not in how
many environments there are.

## Developing a complex app

If an app needs more than the AI Builder and the console give you, develop it
at source level instead of in a second environment: build it locally with
`os dev` and keep it in Git, then [publish it as a package and install
it](/docs/cloud/apps#deploy-from-the-cli) into your environment. See
[Apps](/docs/cloud/apps) for the whole flow.

## Create the environment

On **Environments**, choose **Create Environment** and give it a name; the
platform assigns the address. The address can be changed later with **Change
Hostname** ([Domains](/docs/cloud/domains)). A new environment starts in
**Provisioning** and turns **Active** when its database is ready.

To enter the environment, choose **Open**. It opens the environment's own
console in a new tab, signed in as you.

## The environment page

Each environment has a page with two tabs.

- **Overview** — its address, status, plan and type, the apps installed in it,
  and its access settings. A collapsed line, **Features that need a higher
  plan**, counts what the current plan does not include and lists them when
  opened.
- **Settings** — every action, in five sections: **Apps**, **Plan and
  billing**, **Name and domain**, **Access and sign-in** (including
  [AI agent access](/docs/configure/mcp#on-objectos-cloud)) and **Lifecycle and
  danger zone**.

Actions that change an environment are offered to the organization's owners
and admins.

## Lifecycle

| State | What it means | Your data |
|---|---|---|
| **Active** | Serving requests | — |
| **Suspended** | All runtime traffic is blocked until you resume it | Kept |
| **Archived** | Removed from active views; it no longer starts on a visit | Kept until someone deletes it |
| **Failed** | Provisioning did not complete | None was created |

**Suspend and Resume.** Suspending blocks every request to the environment,
from people and from integrations alike. Nothing is deleted, and **Resume**
brings it back as it was.

**Archive and Restore.** Archiving takes an environment out of use. **Restore**
brings it back, with its data; it starts again on its next visit.

**Delete.** Deleting is a two-step act, so one click can never destroy data:

1. An environment that is not archived yet is **archived**, even if you asked
   for permanent deletion.
2. Deleting an **archived** environment with **Delete permanently if already
   archived** checked destroys its database and all its files at once, with no
   recovery.

Deleting the production environment also asks you to check **Force-delete the
production environment**. An environment whose provisioning failed holds no
data and is deleted at once.

### How long an archived environment is kept

An archived environment is kept, with its database, files and domains, until
someone deletes it. Nothing expires on a timer, on any plan: archiving is
always your choice, and so is deleting. Only the explicit, permanent
**Delete** above destroys the data.
