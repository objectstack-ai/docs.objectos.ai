An **environment** is one running copy of ObjectOS with its own database,
files, apps and address. Your organization's apps live in environments, and
your team signs in to an environment to use them. On ObjectOS Cloud there are
two kinds.

| | **Production** | **Development** |
|---|---|---|
| How many | Exactly one per organization, on every plan | Paid plans only: two, plus one per AI seat, shared across the organization |
| Address | Your organization's slug on the platform domain; can carry [your own domain](/docs/cloud/domains) | A fixed address of the form `dev-<id>`, assigned by the platform; never a branded subdomain or your own domain |
| Idle | Never archived for inactivity | Archived automatically after 7 days without use |
| What it is for | The apps your team uses | Building and trying changes before they reach production |

The **first** environment an organization creates is its production
environment; every one after it is a development environment.

## Who gets which

**Free: one production environment, nothing else.** You build directly in
production. Creating a second environment is refused with
`403 DEV_ENV_PLAN_LOCKED`, and the console offers the upgrade instead.

**Team and above: production plus a pool of development environments.** The
pool is two development environments plus one for each AI seat the
organization pays for, counted across the whole organization. Development
environments are covered by the seat price; they never add a line to the bill.
On a paid plan the usual flow is to build in a development environment, then
[publish it as a package](/docs/cloud/apps#publish-an-environment-as-a-package)
and install that package into production.

Whether development environments exist at all is the plan's decision
(ruled on [cloud#1252](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/cloud/issues/1252));
the one production environment is the same on every plan.

## Create an environment

On **Environments**, choose **Create Environment** and give it a name; the
platform assigns the address. The production environment's address can be
changed later with **Change Hostname** ([Domains](/docs/cloud/domains)); a
development environment keeps the fixed address it was given. A new environment starts
in **Provisioning** and turns **Active** when its database is ready.

To enter an environment, choose **Open**. It opens the environment's own
console in a new tab, signed in as you.

## The environment page

Each environment has a page with two tabs.

- **Overview** — its address, status, plan and type, the apps installed in it,
  and its access settings. A collapsed line, **Features that need a higher
  plan**, counts what the current plan does not include and lists them when
  opened.
- **Settings** — every action, in five sections: **Apps**, **Plan and
  billing**, **Name and domain**, **Access and sign-in** (including
  [AI agent access](/docs/configure/mcp#on-objectos-cloud)) and **Lifecycle and
  danger zone**.

Actions that change an environment are offered to the organization's owners
and admins.

## Lifecycle

| State | What it means | Your data |
|---|---|---|
| **Active** | Serving requests | — |
| **Suspended** | All runtime traffic is blocked until you resume it | Kept |
| **Archived** | Removed from active views; it no longer starts on a visit | Kept until the retention window ends, then deleted |
| **Failed** | Provisioning did not complete | None was created |

**Suspend and Resume.** Suspending blocks every request to the environment,
from people and from integrations alike. Nothing is deleted, and **Resume**
brings it back as it was.

**Archive and Restore.** Archiving takes an environment out of use and starts
its retention clock. **Restore** brings it back within that window; it starts
again on its next visit.

**Delete.** Deleting is a two-step act, so one click can never destroy data:

1. An environment that is not archived yet is **archived**, even if you asked
   for permanent deletion.
2. Deleting an **archived** environment with **Delete permanently if already
   archived** checked destroys its database and all its files at once, with no
   recovery.

Deleting the production environment also asks you to check **Force-delete the
production environment**. An environment whose provisioning failed holds no
data and is deleted at once.

### How long an archived environment is kept

| How it was archived | Kept for |
|---|---|
| By you (**Archive**, or the first step of **Delete**) | At least 30 days; 90 days on Business, a year on Enterprise |
| Automatically, a development environment idle for 7 days | The plan's window: 30 days on Team, 90 on Business, a year on Enterprise |

The environment's owner is warned shortly before an archived environment is
deleted, so there is time to restore it. After the window it is deleted for
good, with its database, files and domains.

### Idle development environments

A development environment that nobody has visited or built in for **7 days**
is archived automatically. Both using the environment and developing in it
(publishing, installing, editing packages) count as activity. The production
environment is never archived this way.

### After a downgrade to Free

A downgrade is not retroactive. Development environments the organization
already runs **keep running**: they are not suspended or archived by the
downgrade. What Free refuses is *adding* one: creating a development
environment, or restoring an archived one, answers `403 DEV_ENV_PLAN_LOCKED`
until the organization upgrades again. An archived development environment is
then kept for Free's window, 7 days. (Ruled on
[cloud#2253](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/cloud/issues/2253) and
[cloud#2204](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/cloud/issues/2204).)
