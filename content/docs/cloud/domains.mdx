An environment on ObjectOS Cloud can be reached at two kinds of address.

| | **Platform subdomain** | **Your own domain** |
|---|---|---|
| Looks like | `acme` on the platform domain | `app.acme.com` |
| Plans | Every plan, Free included | Team and above |
| Environments | Production; a development environment keeps its fixed address | Production only |
| How many | Add as many as you like | One per production environment on Team; more by contract |
| Certificate | Covered by the platform's own | Issued for you once ownership is proven |

The rules on this page were ruled on
[cloud#1087](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/cloud/issues/1087) (platform
subdomains free on every plan) and
[cloud#2136](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/cloud/issues/2136) (your own
domain).

## Platform subdomains

Your production environment's address is your organization's
[slug](/docs/cloud/organization#the-organization-slug) on the platform domain.
It is free on every plan, and so is any other branded label you choose.

To change it, open the environment's **Settings → Name and domain** and choose
**Change Hostname**. Type just the label: the platform domain is added for you.
A label is 4 to 63 lowercase letters, digits and dashes, with no dash at either
end and no two in a row, and a few reserved words are refused. It does not have
to start with your organization's slug.

To add a second platform subdomain instead of replacing the first, use
**Add Domain** in the same section. The address shown on the environment's
**Overview** is the one to give people.

## Your own domain

On **Team** and above, the production environment can also answer at a
subdomain of a domain you own, such as `app.acme.com`.

1. **Add it.** In **Settings → Name and domain**, choose **Add Domain** (or
   **Change Hostname**) and type the full hostname. It must be a subdomain —
   at least three labels. A root domain such as `acme.com` is refused: use
   `www.acme.com` or another subdomain.
2. **Prove you own it.** The domain is created as **Pending Verification**,
   and the console shows a TXT record to publish: name
   `_objectos-challenge.<your hostname>`, with a value unique to this domain.
   It also shows the CNAME target your hostname should point to.
3. **Verify.** Once the TXT record is published, choose **Verify DNS** on the
   domain. ObjectOS Cloud checks the record, then requests the certificate;
   the domain moves through **Verified** and **Provisioning Certificate** to
   **Active**. Choose **Verify DNS** again to move it on if it stops at a step.
4. **Make it primary** (optional). **Set as Primary** decides which address
   sign-in redirects, email links and share links use. The platform subdomain
   keeps working beside it.

Sessions belong to one address: switching between the platform subdomain and
your own domain means signing in again.

### When something goes wrong

| What you see | Why, and what to do |
|---|---|
| `403 CUSTOM_DOMAIN_PLAN_LOCKED` | The organization is on Free. Upgrade to Team. |
| `403 DEV_ENV_DOMAIN_LOCKED` | This is a development environment. Add the domain to the production environment. |
| `400 INVALID_FORMAT`, asking for www or a subdomain | You typed a root domain. Use a subdomain such as `www.acme.com`. |
| `403 CONTACT_SALES_PLAN` | The environment already has its one domain. More than one is an Enterprise arrangement. |
| `409 DUPLICATE_VALUE` | Another environment is already serving that hostname. |
| Verify answers that the TXT record is not visible yet | DNS has not propagated, or the record name or value differs. The answer repeats the record expected. |
| **Failed** | The certificate could not be issued. Check the CNAME and choose **Verify DNS** again; it re-proves ownership first. |

### Downgrade and upgrade

A downgrade **pauses** your own domain; it never releases it. The domain turns
**Suspended**, stops serving, and its certificate is withdrawn, but the record
stays. If it was primary, links fall back to the platform subdomain, which is
unaffected. On any later upgrade the domain is re-provisioned by itself while
your CNAME still points at us. There is no "released after N days".

A suspended domain does not block anyone else who can prove ownership of that
hostname: if another environment proves it, the suspended claim is given up.

### Removing a domain

**Delete** on the domain stops it serving and frees the hostname. Permanently
deleting the environment removes all its domains with it.
