Work on a record rarely happens alone. Every record page can carry the
conversation about it, a trail of what changed, and — when the app is
set up for it — a way to show the record or collect data from people
who don't sign in.

## The discussion

Below the tabs of a record page sits its **Discussion**: comments and
the record's activity in one feed, oldest first. (It appears on objects
whose builder turned discussions on.)

| You can | How |
|---|---|
| Comment | Type in **Write a comment…** and press **Send** or `Ctrl+Enter`. Bold, italic, lists and inline code are in the toolbar, with **Preview** |
| Mention someone | Type `@` and pick a person from the suggestions (or **Mention someone**) |
| Reply | Reply under a comment to keep a thread together |
| React | Add an emoji reaction to a comment |
| Edit or delete | Your own comments only. An edited comment is marked **(edited)** — the server sets that mark, so it can't be hidden |
| Follow the record | **Subscribe to notifications** to hear about this record's updates |

A comment appears the moment you send it, then settles into the saved
copy without a duplicate.

### Filtering the feed

The feed mixes comments with the record's activity. Narrow it with the
filter:

| Filter | Shows |
|---|---|
| **All Activity** | Everything |
| **Comments Only** | Comments and replies |
| **Field Changes** | Edits to the record's fields |
| **Tasks Only** | Task entries |

An empty filter just means the record has nothing of that kind yet.

## @mentions

When you `@mention` someone, they get a notification in their bell with
a preview of the comment and a link straight to the record.

A mention never reaches further than the discussion itself: the person
is notified only if they can read the record and the comment. Mentioning
someone does **not** give them access. The author of the comment isn't
notified about their own mention, and nobody who wasn't mentioned is.

## What changed, and who changed it

| Where | What it shows |
|---|---|
| The discussion feed | For the fields the app's builder chose to track, a line such as **Industry: Technology → Finance**, using the labels you see in the form |
| The **History** tab | The record's full change history. It appears on objects whose builder turned history tracking on |

Changes to fields that aren't tracked don't get a before-and-after line
in the feed.

## Sharing a record with your team

**Share** in the record's `⋯` menu copies the record's address (or opens
your device's share sheet). It doesn't grant anything: the person who
opens it still needs access to the record — see
[Permissions](/docs/configure/permissions).

## Share links: anyone with the link

For objects where the app's builder enabled public sharing, a record can
also have **share links** — a link that shows that one record to people
who don't have an account, or who have one but no access to the record.

A share link is created for one record through the API
(`POST /api/v1/share-links`) by someone who can see that record. Each
link can carry its own limits:

| Limit | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Password** | The page asks for it: **Password required**, then **Wrong password.** until it's right |
| **Signed-in users only** | Visitors without a session see **Sign in required** and a **Sign in** link back to the same page |
| **Email allow-list** | Only signed-in people with a listed email address see the record |
| **Expiry date** | The link stops working on that date |
| **Hidden fields** | Fields left out of what the link shows, on top of the fields the object always hides from share links |

The person who opens the link sees a page outside the app — no menu, no
sign-in unless the link requires one — with the record's fields, read-only.
If fields were hidden, the page says **Some fields are hidden by the
owner** and names them.

### Revoking, and what a link never exposes

- **Revoke** a link (`DELETE /api/v1/share-links/…`) and it stops
  working at once: the page shows **This link has expired or was
  revoked.** So does a link past its expiry date.
- **Delete the record** and every link to it answers **This link is
  invalid or no longer available.** — the same answer as a made-up
  link, so a link never confirms whether a record exists.
- **Hidden fields are removed on the server.** They never reach the
  visitor's browser, not just the screen.
- **One record only.** A link opens exactly the record it was made for,
  never related records or the rest of the list.
- **The password stays on the server.** It's stored hashed and no
  response ever carries it back.
- **Nothing is cached.** Pages served through a link tell browsers and
  proxies not to keep a copy, so the next person on a shared computer
  can't see a password-protected record.
- **You see only your own links.** Listing links returns the ones you
  created, not anyone else's.

If the app's builder turns public sharing off for the object, every link
on it stops working until it's turned back on.

## Public forms: collecting data from outside

A **public form** is a form the app's builder published at a link such
as `/forms/contact-us`, for people who aren't signed in — a contact
form, a web-to-lead or a support request. Visitors fill it in and see
the form's confirmation (a thank-you message, for example); each
submission becomes a record your team works on like any other.

What a public form never opens up:

- **Only the listed fields.** Visitors see and can fill only the fields
  the form shows — not the rest of the object.
- **No looking things up.** A public form has no fields that search
  your records (no lookup or people pickers), whatever the form declares.
- **No forged ownership.** A visitor can't set who owns the record,
  which organization it belongs to, or fields such as its status;
  values sent for those are dropped and the server sets them.

To **close** a public form, the builder unpublishes it or turns off
anonymous access. The link then answers as if the form doesn't exist,
and submissions stop immediately.
